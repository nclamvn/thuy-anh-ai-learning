Adult rehearsal in three stages

Use adults and synthetic stories for this rehearsal; do not invite children or use real personal stories or identifying details. Assign a storyteller, an operator and an observer where possible; with two adults, record the combined roles. These are actions to try, not measured results. Adult actions must be observed directly; software does not role-play the people or automatically validate a rehearsal.

Tell the idea

Tell: the storyteller chooses an idea; retain the exact words and language. Explicitly choose a conversation band without inferring ability from age. Adult notes are optional and separate. Speaking, writing or paper drawing are alternatives; the storyteller checks any transcription.

Review and build

Review and build: open suggestions only through an explicit action. Compare original words, selected choices and unsupported wishes. Ask what to keep, reject or change; silence is not agreement. Artwork name and narration are written or chosen by the person; method copy must not automatically become a title. Confirmation commits an in-session blueprint only. Return to necessary unresolved choices rather than confirming on behalf of the storyteller.

Look back and change

Look back and change: after an actual render, compare the scene with the original words and confirmed choices. Invite a one-detail revision or no change; record before/after versions if available. Reflection is optional, not a compulsory answer for progression. Keep an unrendered wish, choose paper, return or stop. Save JSON before close/reload where needed; mark saving unverified if no file is observed.

  • The observer records exact actions/words; do not infer intent from silence, appearance or template choice.
  • A rescue is intervention for interaction/technical failure; distinguish it from ordinary facilitation. Record who did what, why and what data remained.
  • Every observation is manual and unverified; software does not automatically pass the rehearsal.

Original words → choices → scene sheet

Leave fields blank before rehearsal. Use one row per detail: exact original words; machine/adult proposal; keep, reject or change decision; rendered scene or not observed; and supporting action/words. A beautiful picture is not evidence of authorship.

  • Observation status: observed / did not occur in this attempt / not observed. Use “did not occur” only when an opportunity existed and record that opportunity.
  • Word origin: original story, revision, adult notes, prepared proposal or unverified import. Preserve labels; do not attribute adult words to a learner.
  • Choice outcome: keep / reject / change / unrendered wish / paper / stop / not chosen. Accepting an omission means accepting that it is not rendered, not that it was fulfilled.
  • Each M1–M6 has its own status and evidence field; all may remain not observed. Existing pilot thresholds remain proposals, not rehearsal results.
  • Record failures, rescue count/actions, durations if measured, printed version and needed changes. Keep null when unknown; do not insert zero as though measured.

Eight synthetic workflow scenarios

These are team-authored synthetic examples, not learner words or trial results. Adults select one and record what actually happens; do not prefill PASS. Tasks are observation criteria, not a promise that every phrasing is understood.

  • S01 · Whale with supported choices. Synthetic words: I want a teal whale carrying a small city. At night there are gardens and a star bridge. Task: Compare all five choices, original words and scene; render only after review/confirmation.
  • S02 · Wish outside the template. Synthetic words: I want a dragon carrying a candy shop. Task: Keep dragon/candy shop visibly unrendered; no silent whale substitution. Try paper, return or stop.
  • S03 · Conflicting sky. Synthetic words: I want a whale carrying a city. The sky is both night and sunset. Task: The conflict requires an explicit choice; do not resolve by confirming for someone or closing a panel.
  • S04 · Negating a supported choice. Synthetic words: I want a whale carrying a city, with no gardens and no star bridge. Task: Check the negation and both false choices; if unclear to the system, correct manually and review.
  • S05 · Reject a suggestion. Synthetic words: I want a whale carrying a city at night. Task: Preview then reject a proposal; record preserved data and non-adoption, without treating the proposal as artwork.
  • S06 · Change one detail after print. Synthetic words: Keep the star bridge; change the sky from night to sunset. Task: Use after an actual printed world with a bridge. Compare before/after, preserve other choices and reconfirm; record a version only if rendered.
  • S07 · Choose paper. Synthetic words: Today I want to draw a dragon on paper. Task: Paper is valid; record renderer not tested rather than switching to the template to complete a trial.
  • S08 · Choose to stop. Synthetic words: I do not want to print yet. I want a break. Task: Stop without scoring; record words/actions if agreed, not automatic failure or renderer validation.
